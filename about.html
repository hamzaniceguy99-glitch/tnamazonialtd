The backup nobody has restored

Almost every business has backups. Far fewer have ever watched one come back.

How we work

Boring controls, tested properly

Cyber security sold to small businesses tends to arrive as a product. The controls that actually prevent incidents are duller and cheaper: patch what you run, stop people working as administrators, turn off what nobody uses, and disable the accounts of staff who left. Cyber Essentials is essentially a checklist of that, which is why most of the work is configuration rather than purchasing.

Backups are the clearest example of a control everyone believes they have. Having a backup and having a restore are different things, and ransomware encrypts whatever it can reach — including the backup drive plugged into the server. Testing a restore in front of the client, and verifying one copy is genuinely unreachable, changes an assumption into a fact.

Phishing simulations deserve care too. Run to catch people out, they teach staff that mistakes are punished, so the next real click gets hidden for a day. Run to build reporting, they shorten the only interval that matters: the gap between the click and somebody telling you.
